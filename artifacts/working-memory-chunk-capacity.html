Working Memory / CapacityEntry No. 001

The most famous number in psychology is off by almost half.

7≈ 4

"The magic number seven, plus or minus two" has been taught in classrooms for almost 70 years. When later researchers designed tests that stopped people from silently rehearsing or grouping items, the real number of things you can hold in mind at once turned out to be closer to four.

30-second version: Your working memory holds about four independent "slots," not seven. Experts don't get more slots — they get better at packing more into each one. That packing trick is called chunking, and it's why phone numbers have dashes in them.

9 independent items — well past a 4-item capacity.

Chess grandmasters do the same thing with board positions: a beginner sees dozens of individual pieces, a grandmaster sees a handful of familiar patterns — openings, formations, threats — each pattern occupying one slot instead of one piece per slot. The slot count doesn't change with expertise. What fits in each slot does.
